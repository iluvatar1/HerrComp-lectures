Basic slicing (a[start:stop:step]) always returns a view — same underlying memory, just a different way of looking at it. Anything that needs to pick non-contiguous or reordered elements — a list/array of indices, or a boolean mask — can't be described as a simple stride, so NumPy has to build a new array: a copy. That's the rule the lecture's a[[2,3,2,4,2]] and a[mask] examples both follow, even though it's never stated outright there.
